### Likely wrong turns

- **P1 or P3 ticks "Only jobs I'd apply to" and quotes the filtered row** (WP-T6-01).
  - Example answer: "gRPC, 3 of the jobs I'd apply to, Ember Payments wants it." Ember
    Payments is in the unfiltered company list, but its job has a fit of 3.5, below the
    filter's threshold of 4.
  - The answer would still meet the success criterion, because the company check passes.
    The evidence behind it is wrong for the filter the user chose.
  - Recovery: untick the box. The numbers become consistent again. Nothing tells the user
    to do this.
- **P3 opens "Strengthen" or "Most asked for".**
  - Strengthen leads with "1. Terraform · Asked for in 31 postings · Your CV: Partly".
    "Most asked for" leads with PostgreSQL, Kubernetes and Go, all "Has it". gRPC is the
    first "Missing" row there, at #8.
  - A P3 who reasons "I only partly have Terraform and 31 postings ask for it" gives a
    defensible answer that is outside `lists.learn`.
  - Not filed. The view descriptions ("skills your CV shows only partly", "every skill,
    whether or not you have it") are clear, and Learn next is the default.
- **P3 clicks "Improve the analysis" to make the numbers trustworthy.**
  - The cost is stated next to the button ("reads 36 postings with Claude · about 4
    sessions · uses your Claude plan"). It starts at once, with a progress bar and
    "Improving…".
  - When it finishes, the announcement says "Done: Skills analysis improved: every posting
    read by Claude".
  - The basis line still says "last read Sep 25" and now reads "66 read by Claude, 0 by
    quick rules" against "69 postings" (WP-T6-03). The ranking does not change, so the
    answer is unaffected.
- **P3 follows a fit-report link** ("says who?").
  - The report opens, for example "Driftwood Analytics — Backend Engineer (Go) · Fit 3.4
    / 5 … Gaps it found: React, gRPC, Python", which is good corroboration.
  - The only in-page way out is "← Applications". Browser Back returns to Skills with the
    disclosure closed (WP-T6-04).
- **P2 opens the "Missing ▾" menu next to a skill, thinking it filters.**
  - Choosing "Has it" gives "Saved: your CV has gRPC. **Undo**". The row stays where it
    is, now reading "Has it".
  - Undo restores it. Harmless (F-069 is closed).

## Findings

### WP-T6-01: "Only jobs I'd apply to" changes only the headline count; the breakdown, companies, gap list and ranking stay on all 69 postings
- **Severity:** 3
- **Where:** Skills › Learn next (and Strengthen, Most asked for) › "Only jobs I'd apply to (fit 4 and up)" › every skill row and its evidence
- **Personas:** P3 (the evidence persona: "says who?", "which companies want this?"), P1 and P2 (both ticked the equivalent filter in M6)
- **Step/Q:** optional step, Q4
- **Method:** walkthrough (M7 re-run)
- **What goes wrong:**
  - **The row contradicts itself.** "Asked for in **3** good-fit postings (12 required, 10
    nice to have)". In "Most asked for" it is "Asked for in 4 good-fit postings (33
    required, 7 nice to have)". The two parts of one line count different sets of
    postings.
  - **The ranking ignores the filtered numbers.**
    - The order stays the all-postings order. Under the line "ranked by how many postings
      ask for them", "3. GCP · 1 good-fit postings" sits above "5. Machine Learning · 2"
      and "7. Datadog · 2".
    - In "Most asked for", Go (6) is #3 below PostgreSQL (4).
    - The bars are drawn from the filtered numbers, so GCP's bar is visibly shorter than
      Machine Learning's two rows below it.
  - **The evidence is not filtered.**
    - "Show the evidence" lists the same nine companies and six fit reports as without
      the filter. They include Ember Payments (fit 3.5) and Driftwood Analytics —
      Backend Engineer (Go) #23 (fit 3.4, Rejected).
    - The user cannot see which 3 good-fit postings ask for gRPC. Anyone who quotes a
      company may name one outside the filter they chose.
  - **The filter persists.** It applies to all three views and survives navigation within
    the session.
  - **Why 3.** This is the control a user reaches for to make the evidence relevant to
    their own search. Two of three M6 personas used it. Once ticked, the screen misleads
    about which jobs back the numbers and contradicts its own ranking rule, exactly when
    the user is asking "says who?". `ia.md` §2.6 asks only that the filter "shows what it
    changed". It does not say which parts of a row follow the filter, so this is a design
    gap and not only a prototype shortcut.
  - **Why not 4.** The top three and their companies still meet the success criterion,
    and unticking restores consistent numbers.
  - This escalates F-056 (2): its feedback half is fixed (the status line), but the
    "counts mix" half is now more visible.
- **Evidence:** app/ux/design/evidence/WP-T6-01.png (full page, filter on, gRPC evidence open)
- **Reproduction:**
  1. Open http://127.0.0.1:4420/prototypes/T6.html and click Reset prototype.
  2. Click "Skills" in the nav.
  3. Tick "Only jobs I'd apply to (fit 4 and up)".
  4. Observe "Showing counts from the 14 postings with a fit of 4 or more." and row 1
     "Asked for in 3 good-fit postings (12 required, 10 nice to have)".
  5. Observe "3. GCP · Asked for in 1 good-fit postings" above "5. Machine Learning ·
     Asked for in 2 good-fit postings" and "7. Datadog · 2".
  6. Click "Show the evidence for gRPC". Observe the same nine companies and six fit
     reports as with the box unticked.
  7. Optional: click "Most asked for" and read "1. PostgreSQL · Asked for in 4 good-fit
     postings (33 required, 7 nice to have)".

## Verdict

**A first-time user completes T6 unaided, in two actions: Skills, then "Show the
evidence for gRPC".**

- Learn next opens by default. Row 1 reads "gRPC · Asked for in 22 postings (12
  required, 10 nice to have) · Your CV: Missing".
- The basis line says the numbers come from 69 postings at 12 companies, read by Claude
  or by quick rules.
- The disclosure names nine companies and six of the user's own fit reports that flag
  gRPC as a gap.
- All three parts of the goal, and where the numbers come from, are on one screen.

**The step most likely to mislead is ticking "Only jobs I'd apply to (fit 4 and up)"
(WP-T6-01, severity 3).**

- Only the headline count follows the filter. The required/nice-to-have breakdown, the
  companies, the fit-report list and the ranking stay on all 69 postings.
- An evidence-seeking user (P3, and P1 and P2 as observed in M6) gets numbers that
  contradict each other on the same line, and companies that are not among the jobs they
  chose.
- The success criterion is still met through the company check, so this does not stop
  completion. It misleads exactly the "judge where the numbers come from" part of the
  task.

**Because a severity-3 issue was found, T6 does not pass M7's "no severity-3 or -4"
acceptance bar as walked.** The other findings are severity 2 or less.
