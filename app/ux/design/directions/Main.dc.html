Job Search Console · M7 design directions

Three ways to organise the console

Each direction draws the same three hard flows from the M6 study — first run (T1), recovering a failed check (T8), and getting a tailored CV and cover letter (T5, which starts from the applications list). All data is fictional (Alex Rivera). Press Play on any flow to click through it.

A · Workbench

Organised by object. A side menu lists the things you own — Applications, To review, Companies you follow, Skills, My CV — and an Activity tray stays docked on every page. Opening a job shows it in a side panel next to the list.

Compact, neutral, system sans. Dense tables for the career-ops user.

T1 · First runSet-up checklist on the empty Applications page T8 · Failed checkActivity tray → plain cause → Try again T5 · CV and letterApplications list → side panel → Documents

B · Stages

Organised by workflow stage. The top bar is the job hunt itself — Find → Decide → Apply → Track — with your tools (My CV, Skills, Companies) on the right. Each job moves through the stages; each stage shows only what you do there.

Warm paper, serif headings, roomy cards. Reads like a guided process.

T1 · First runThree-step set-up before the stages open T8 · Failed checkFailure shown in the Decide stage, retried in place T5 · CV and letterTrack list → Apply stage checklist

C · Today

Organised around next actions. You land on Today: a short list of what needs you (a failed check, new openings, a recruiter screen coming up), each with its one action. Full workspaces — Applications, Openings, My CV, Skills, Companies — open from there and from search.

Bold, high-contrast, larger type. One thing at a time for the first-timer.

T1 · First runToday becomes a set-up list on day one T8 · Failed checkThe failure is the first card on Today T5 · CV and letterApplications workspace → job → documents
